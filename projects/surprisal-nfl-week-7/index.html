What’s a bit?

A bit measures surprise. A coin landing heads, a 1-in-2 chance, is 1 bit. Each extra bit halves the chance: 2 bits is 1 in 4, 3 bits is 1 in 8, and a sure thing is 0 bits. The chances here come from each game’s pregame betting line. The line gave the Giants about a 1-in-5 chance, so their win scores 2.35 bits.

    Method

    How scoring works

    Every final gets two scores, both in bits. Bits have no units, so a moneyline upset and a blowout against the spread land on the same scale.

    01

    Start from the line

    Turn each price into a probability, then strip the book’s margin. Thursday’s Patriots–Bears line:

    Bears −148148 ÷ 248 = 59.7%
    Patriots +124100 ÷ 224 = 44.6%
    sum104.3% → scale to 100%
    no-vigBears 57.2% · Pats 42.8%
    02

    Result surprise

    surprise = −log₂ p(what happened)

    The Bears won: −log₂ 0.572 = 0.81 bits. A Patriots win would have scored 1.22. Each halving of the probability adds one bit:

    50%1.00
    25%2.00
    10%3.32
    5%4.32
    1%6.64
    Why this is Bayesian surprise

    Bayesian surprise is the KL divergence from your beliefs before to your beliefs after. Once the final settles who won, “after” is certain, and that KL collapses to −log₂ p. For win/loss, the two are the same number.

    03

    Margin surprise

    The spread sets an expected margin, and NFL finals scatter around it with a steady SD of about 13.5 points (Stern, 1991, and later replications).

    margin = −log₂ P(miss ≥ observed)

    Panthers −3, lost by 30: a 2.4σ miss with P ≈ 1.5%, so 6.11 bits. This lens catches blowouts that the result lens scores as only mildly surprising.

    04

    Chaos index

    A line’s expected surprise is its entropy: what a calibrated market should average. Divide what happened by what the lines expected. For the 13 finals of Week 7:

    result12.7 ÷ 12.1 bits = 1.05×
    margin17.5 ÷ 18.8 bits = 0.93×

    For margins the expectation is 1.44 bits a game under a normal model: if the line is calibrated, the chance of a bigger miss is uniform between 0 and 1, and the average of −log₂ of a uniform draw is 1/ln 2. By both measures, Week 7 went about as the lines expected. A few games carried most of the surprise.

    05

    Updating team strength

    Add a prior on each matchup’s true gap (τ) next to game-to-game noise (σ), and the KL update measures how much a final should change your view of the teams. Within one sport it ranks games exactly like the margin lens. The size depends on τ, which you would fit from how lines move week to week.

    With τ = 3 and σ = 13.5, the Buccaneers’ rout moved the gap 1.6 points: 0.19 bits. Single games are weak evidence.
